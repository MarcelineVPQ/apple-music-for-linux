The lyrics as the stage: seven ideas to judge

Design only. Nothing here is in the app and no app code changed. Each page is built on Sonata's real theme (theme.js tokens, palette.js album colours, Glass), with light interaction so you can feel it: press play, flip pages, switch options. Sample content: traditional public-domain songs and a fictional band; photos from Wikimedia Commons (see CREDITS.md).

Top bar · album colour, Dark / Light, Glass, play What to judge · the numbered notes, matched by pins on the design Notes switch · hides the pins for a clean look Space · play / pause
01

Stage view

Lyrics as the centrepiece

The lyrics window grows into the now-playing screen: big lyrics in the middle, the album and transport on the left, Up Next and the playlist on the right, with room for the sheet-notes staff and the montage.

What to judgeDo the lyrics own the window while "next song", "track 13 of 24" and the playlist stay a glance away? Does the shrink-back to today's lyrics window feel like the same thing?
02

Sheet notes in the lyrics +1

Live sheet notes

Real data with sound: the vocadito "Clementine" voice, with the prototype's notes and pitch. First play draws the pitch line as it's heard; later plays show the notes on a staff with syllables.

What to judgeBand on top, or riding over the sung line? Is the first play's pitch line worth showing, and is the notation the right amount of music?
03

Karaoke mode

After 0.15.10: karaoke

Full-screen, night-stage karaoke: a melody lane from the +1 pitch line, the sung line big with a word-by-word wipe, the next singer waiting, countdown dots on the coming singer's side.

What to judgeDuet sides and colours carried over from the lyrics; the countdown at the intro and the fiddle break; does the melody lane help or distract?
04

Backing vocals

Smaller lines under the main line

Three options side by side, same moment: A, an own line under a rule; B, under the word it answers; C, shown only on cue with its room kept.

What to judgePick one (or a blend): calm and readable (A), the timing visible (B), or the least clutter (C). Check it in Light too.
05

Ken Burns montage

Photos behind the lyrics

Venue photos drift behind the lyrics window with a theme-colour shade and the photo credit in the footer, plus the new option as it would sit in Settings.

What to judgeCalm enough to read over? The shade rises per photo until the words pass 4.5:1: in Light mode that can mean a lot of shade. Off by default, frozen on pause.
06

Liner notes

Album experience

A CD booklet you page through: cover and disc, editorial notes, the recording date and venue with the setlist, the venue's Wikipedia article and photo, personnel and per-track credits, lyrics pages, sources and the back cover.

What to judgeThe booklet metaphor and the page turn; the order of the sections; every block's source chip; the lyrics pages marking the line being sung.
07

Radio show

Apple Music Radio talk segments

The lyrics window during a talk segment: live captions of the hosts from local speech-to-text, coloured and sided like duets; the show card with hosts; the songs played so far; On air, with a visualizer or montage behind.

What to judgeDo duet colours read as "who's talking"? Is the played list and timeline useful during a show? Visualizer, montage or plain behind the captions? (Placeholder hosts and dialogue.)
Not judged here: the data plumbing (Wikidata matching, MusicBrainz, setlist.fm keys, speech-to-text model choice), performance budgets (the motion is transform and opacity only, frozen when paused) and the per-locale wording (Sony-style glossary rules apply when built).
